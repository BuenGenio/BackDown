BackDown indexes your drives, finds what's duplicated across all of them, and tells you, with a SHA-256 hash behind every claim, which drives are safe to wipe. It recognises what things are and where they came from, files old drives into one archive, keeps mirrors, watches your drives' health, and gets files back from failing or emptied drives. It runs on your own computer and only ever reads the drives it looks at, except when you ask it to copy into an archive, keep a mirror, or free up a drive.

New here? Start with [Getting started](/docs/getting-started/): install it, open it, index two drives, see what's duplicated, and ask whether a drive is safe to wipe. [Concepts](/docs/concepts/) explains the ideas the rest of the docs rely on.

> [!NOTE]
> These docs describe BackDown 1.0.0-beta.6: the desktop app, its background service and the `backdown` command, on Linux, macOS and Windows. It's a beta, so some things will change before 1.0; the [status page](/status/) has what's released and what's next.
