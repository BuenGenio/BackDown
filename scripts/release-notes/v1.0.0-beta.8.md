The eighth beta of BackDown 1.0: your photos and videos organise themselves by where and when they were taken — country, city, nearest airport and date, all worked out offline — and you can find them by place, on the command line and in a new Places view. It also carries beta.7's seconds-fast search by name across every machine.

## Install

| Platform | Installer | Command line |
|---|---|---|
| Linux x86-64 | `backdown-io_1.0.0-beta.8_amd64.deb` (Debian, Ubuntu) or `.AppImage` | `backdown-v1.0.0-beta.8-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` (static) |
| Linux ARM64 | `backdown-io_1.0.0-beta.8_arm64.deb` or `.AppImage` | `backdown-v1.0.0-beta.8-linux-arm64.tar.gz` (static) |
| macOS (Apple silicon + Intel) | `BackDown-1.0.0-beta.8.dmg` | `backdown-v1.0.0-beta.8-macos-universal.tar.gz` |

Windows builds are paused for this beta while the port is finished; follow it at <https://github.com/BuenGenio/BackDown/issues/94>.

**This release is signed.** `SHA256SUMS.txt.sig` is an Ed25519 signature of `SHA256SUMS.txt` by BackDown's release key, and `backdown update` checks it before installing anything. To check by hand, see [Air-gapped computers](https://backdown.io/docs/guides/air-gapped/).

The installers and apps themselves still aren't signed by Apple. On macOS, right-click the app and choose Open the first time (for the command-line binary: `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine backdown`).

## What's new since beta.6

### Your photos, organised by place and time

BackDown can now read what a photo or video records about itself and turn it into tags you can browse and search by — without sending anything anywhere.

- **`backdown tag exif <folder>`** reads each photo and video's own metadata (EXIF for stills, QuickTime for video): the day it was taken and, where present, the GPS position.
- **`backdown tag geo`** turns those positions into a `country → region → city` hierarchy and the nearest airport (FLL, CDG, HKG…). This is fully offline: trimmed GeoNames and OurAirports tables are built into the binary, and a coordinate is matched by great-circle distance — no map service, and the reference data never touches your index.
- **`backdown place add "Foreign Correspondents Club" --at 22.281,114.156`** names a place, and `backdown place apply` tags every photo taken within it.
- **`backdown tag find`** finds content by where and when: everything within a distance of an airport, a town or a position, filtered by tags and a date range, newest or nearest first — with `--paths` and `--json` for scripts.

### A Places view in the app

The browser and the app now show this. The **Places** sidebar has a search box that suggests places, tags and years; an offline map drawn from the built-in places (no tiles fetched) with photo clusters and the search circle; tag counts to narrow by; and a grid grouped by distance or month where you can tag, untag, or set keep/drop/reviewed in bulk. Reading a drive's photos is a new stoppable job you can watch in Places and Activity.

Underneath is a small model layer — tags (facts about content), flags (decisions about an item) and edges (relationships) — keyed by content, so a tag follows a file across copies and moves. See `backdown tag`, `flag` and `edge`.

### Search answers in seconds, across every machine

Carried over from beta.7 (which was never released on its own): a name index finds matches in a fraction of a second however big the index, and the search box's **Everywhere** covers your other machines too, this computer's matches first. After upgrading, the service builds the index once as its own job (about 8 minutes for 27 million files); search works as before until it's done.

## Known limitations

- The new reference data adds about 2.6 MB to the binary (GeoNames, CC BY 4.0; OurAirports, public domain — credited in `backdown tag geo --about`).
- Reverse-geocoding is to the nearest populated place of 1,000+ people; a photo far from any town takes the nearest one, with lower confidence. The named-place and airport matches are within a set distance.
- The name index takes some room: on 8.5 million distinct names, about 1.2 GB.
- Searching another machine at its fastest needs beta.8 there too.
- The installers aren't signed or notarised by Apple.
- Windows builds are paused this beta while the port is finished (#94); Linux and macOS are unaffected.
- Pro, Team and Forensic features are free during the beta; pricing may change before 1.0.
